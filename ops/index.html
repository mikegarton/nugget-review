Limits & burn

Each row names the resource being consumed and the consumer. Projected at reset = share of quota used ÷ share of the period elapsed — a straight-line projection of where the period ends. blue <50% · green 50–75% · yellow 75–90% · orange 90–100% · red ≥100%. Types: expires = prepaid allotment, unused value is lost at reset; ceiling = do-not-exceed cap; metered = pay-per-use judged against a chosen budget — nothing blocks at it. The tick on each bar marks where "exactly on pace" would be right now. Campaign yield by query

Prime indicators first (nug/item · nug/$ · nug/kw — cost is every call cost of the videos that query found), then the funnel: found → screened out / promoted → processed → nuggets → your ratings. A query with finds but no ≥4★ after a couple of weeks is a candidate for stopping (the stop-phrase button on its headers in the viewer). Status: searching or stopped.

Sources — priority & economics

One integer per method of acquisition, lower = processed sooner, ties allowed; the queue tie-break is newest-published. Manual adds jump to priority 0 for the first 3 per hour. Priority is editable here with the ops write key (the priority button). Sources are subscribed or discovered — provenance, not quality; nuggets are rated on merit.

Category tags — coverage & TTL

Nuggets carry ordered category tags, primary first (since 2026-08-06; older nuggets show their legacy type instead). Primary is the tag in position one — the only tag counted where totals must equal sums, like cost attribution. Coverage = share of categorized nuggets carrying the tag in any position; because tags are multiple, coverage sums exceed 100% by design. A tag above ~60% coverage presumptively isn't earning its keep — unless it stratifies (rare on most sources, dominant on a few; filter on it in the viewer and see whether it separates channels). Human call, not a rule; the yellow figure is the flag. TTL is the tag's soft-expiry window: unrated nuggets outlive the longest TTL among their tags and drop out of "open" (rows kept — the viewer's Expired chip lists them; rating a nugget exempts it for good). TTL edits are SQL-editor only: update yt_categories set ttl_days = … where key = '…';

Channel economics — lifetime

Columns: identity, then prime value rates, then quality scores, then volume. Cost = Claude tokens + prorated Supadata credits, from the call log. Nugget counts are surviving nuggets. Disabled channels are campaign discoveries or manual-add hosts, not part of the subscribed poll list. Value rates are per-dollar — nug/$ and 5★/$ — because substack costs run magnitudes below video costs and "nuggets per dollar" compares cleanly where "$0.00003 per nugget" reads as noise; bigger is better, like every density column. nug/mo = surviving nuggets ÷ months since the channel's first processed item (denominator floored at 1 month, like views/day's 1-day floor). taste = the AI's guess at your interest (0–100); mentor = the AI's outside-expert value-per-time score (0–100); pk/med/avg = peak, median, average over surviving nuggets. hype (−5…+5) is per scored video, including zero-nugget ones. Substack transcript/minutes columns are legitimately blank; nug/item and nug/kw (nuggets per 1,000 words of transcript or post) are the density metrics both sources share — nug/kw counts only items processed since word counting began (2026-07-30), on both sides of the ratio. Channels processed before 2026-07-25 predate cost logging and show $0 (per-dollar rates blank). The ratios are computed in the database views since 2026-09-22. Subscribe appears on YouTube channels that are not subscribed; the two unsub buttons on subscribed channels: nuggets stops the feed, YouTube unsubscribes the account.

Pipeline parameters — yt_params

Every live number the pipeline runs on, grouped by what it governs; the next run picks changes up, no redeploy. The bold name is the row's key — search the page or the code for it verbatim.
